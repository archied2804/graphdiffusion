\section{Results}

\subsection{Preliminary: representation studies}

Radial diffusion on canonical shapes established the representation and connectivity that were
carried unchanged into the aerodynamic setting. A preliminary circle-series study denoised a radial
field $r(\theta)$ on a boundary ring graph and confirmed that the pipeline reproduces target shape
distributions: sampling from $x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ recovers the family of Fourier-perturbed
unit circles it was trained on, with low distributional discrepancy and no boundary violations.
That study also fixed the two design choices reused downstream: a single-scalar radial node feature
$[r]$ rather than richer coupled channels, and higher-order ring-neighbour connectivity, which
improved distribution fidelity sharply over nearest-neighbour-only graphs. This subsection is
scaffolding for the aerodynamic results that follow, not a headline in its own right.

  benchmark: the reverse process recovers the target family of Fourier-perturbed boundaries from

\subsection{Surface-quality methodology study}

Boundary roughness is a sampling-time property, not a training-loss property. A dedicated
surface-quality study tried five orthogonal levers to clean up the visibly wobbly boundaries of the
baseline conditional model, each evaluated on the same four conditioning targets by the mean
second-difference roughness of $r(\theta)$ (lower is smoother). The outcome separates the two
levers that act on the reverse process from three ``modern DDPM tricks'' that act on the training
objective.

% provisional
\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
    \toprule
    Configuration & Diffusion steps $T$ & Overall roughness & $\Delta$ vs baseline & Best val loss \\
    \midrule
    baseline                                        & 200  & 0.03379            & ---       & 0.00306   \\
    longer training                                 & 200  & 0.02125            & $-$37\%   & 0.000104  \\
    longer training $+$ $T=1000$ steps              & 1000 & \textbf{0.01523}   & $-$55\%   & 0.000090  \\
    $+$ Min-SNR-$\gamma$ loss \cite{hang2023minsnr} & 200  & 0.02700            & $-$20\%   & \textbf{0.000028} \\
    $+$ EMA \cite{song2020improved}                 & 200  & 0.03747            & $+$11\%   & 0.000124  \\
    $+$ EMA $+$ v-prediction \cite{salimans2022progressive} & 200 & 0.02451     & $-$27\%   & 0.000189  \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Surface-quality sweep: roughness and validation loss for five training/sampling
  configurations. Lower roughness is smoother; bold marks the winner in each column.}
  \label{tab:surface-quality}
\end{table}

The $T=1000$ configuration wins, cutting roughness 55\% to 0.01523, yet its best validation loss is
essentially identical to longer-training-alone (0.000090 vs 0.000104). The gain therefore lives in
the reverse process --- more reverse steps mean more polishing in the low-noise regime where fine
geometric detail emerges --- and not in a better-fitted network. The three training-objective tricks
are a validated negative result: the lowest validation loss of the whole sweep (Min-SNR-$\gamma$ at
0.000028) yields \emph{worse} roughness (0.02700) than simply training longer, and EMA actually
pushes roughness above the baseline. Minimising the training MSE harder does not smooth boundaries,
which is the central methodological finding carried into the validation work below.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{report_roughness_metric.png}
  \caption{The surface-roughness diagnostic on a real raw versus Fourier-smoothed generated boundary.
  Left: the radial profiles $r(\theta)$ overlay closely, the raw curve carrying small high-frequency
  wobble on the smooth trend. Right: the per-node second difference $|\Delta^2 r(\theta)|$ isolates
  that wobble --- mean roughness falls from $0.0117$ (raw) to $0.0046$ (smoothed) --- the quantity the
  surface-quality study minimises.}

\end{figure}

\subsection{CFD solver validation gate}

Before trusting the loop on generated shapes, we gate it on \emph{known} dataset ellipses: the
independent solver reproduces the dataset's own $C_p$ at correlation up to 0.998. This establishes
that the grading physics is sound before any generated geometry is judged by it.

% provisional: pending validation-framework re-verification
\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lccccc}
    \toprule
    Sim & Re & $C_p$ correlation & affine slope $a$ & offset $b$ \\
    \midrule
    sim\_0    & 500  & \textbf{0.998} & 1.78 & $\approx 0$ \\
    sim\_2850 & 754  & 0.93           & 1.55 & $\approx 0$ \\
    sim\_5700 & 1000 & 0.92           & 1.70 & $\approx 0$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{CFD solver validation gate on three known dataset ellipses spanning the Reynolds-number
  range: $C_p$ correlation, affine slope, and offset.}
  \label{tab:solver-gate}
\end{table}

The solver reproduces the dataset curve up to a single multiplicative normalisation $a \approx 1.78$,
strikingly close to the dataset's documented ``u / 1.8'' velocity scaling --- a credible analytic
explanation rather than a solver artefact. Correlation tapers slightly with Reynolds number through
genuine under-resolution of the stronger vortex shedding, not a correctness failure. The decisive
engineering fix concerned stability: the instantaneous force trace showed sustained $\pm 15\%$
density ``breathing'', diagnosed as a domain quarter-wave acoustic resonance trapped by the
reflecting boundaries. Adding a sponge / absorbing layer \cite{xu2013sponge} together with
rest-initialisation collapsed the drag-coefficient noise from $\pm 1.5$ to $\pm 0.05$ and
\emph{improved} the $C_p$ match (0.98 $\to$ 0.998), confirming the earlier ``$C_d$ noise'' was
never physics.

\subsection{Inverse-design validation}

Generated shapes hit their target $C_p$ at correlation $\approx 0.95$ in-distribution, verified by
the independent solver rather than the model's own pressure head --- the first independent
confirmation of the inverse-design claim. Each generated shape is run through the validated loop and
its measured $C_p$ correlated against the target it was conditioned on.

  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lllccc}
    \toprule
    Target & Type & Re & $C_p$ corr (raw) & $C_p$ corr (smoothed) \\
    \midrule
    \#4849    & in-distribution     & 960 & \textbf{0.964} & 0.952 \\
    \#4066    & in-distribution     & 884 & \textbf{0.966} & 0.963 \\
    synthetic & out-of-distribution & 754 & \textbf{0.860} & 0.876 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Inverse-design validation: solver-measured $C_p$ correlation against the conditioning
  target for two in-distribution and one out-of-distribution shape.}
  \label{tab:inverse-design}
\end{table}

In-distribution the loop confirms inverse design at correlation $\approx 0.95$. Across a
five-target out-of-distribution spread --- perturbing the dataset-mean $C_p$ in amplitude, sign,
frequency, and tilt --- correlation falls to 0.70--0.93, declining with distance from the training
set, exactly the extrapolation failure the loop was built to surface. Surface roughness turns out to
be cosmetic: raw versus Fourier-smoothed variants differ by only $\Delta\mathrm{corr} \sim 0.01$ in
$C_p$ and $\sim 5\%$ in $C_d$, so the characteristic wobble does not corrupt the physics.

\begin{quote}
  Correlation (morphology) is the inverse-design verdict. The model captures $C_p$ \emph{shape},
  not absolute amplitude: the affine slope on generated targets (2.2--3.6) exceeds the dataset
  factor ($\approx 1.78$), a known calibration limitation rather than a pass/fail failure.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Out-of-distribution limits, magnitude creep, and the posterior-guidance negative}

Posterior guidance is an unambiguous negative out-of-distribution. The natural lever for the OOD
gap is Diffusion Posterior Sampling \cite{chung2023dps}, which adds a likelihood gradient from the
model's own pressure head at each reverse step. Swept on the single synthetic OOD target --- same
seed, so the shapes differ \emph{only} by the guidance --- and graded by the independent solver,
every metric degrades.

% provisional: pending validation-framework re-verification
\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lccc}
    \toprule
    DPS weight & $C_p$ corr & affine slope $a$ & $C_d$ \\
    \midrule
    0.0 (plain CFG) & \textbf{0.860} & 3.46 & 1.59 \\
    0.3             & 0.818          & 5.22 & 2.46 \\
    0.5             & 0.807          & 5.32 & 2.33 \\
    1.0             & 0.762          & 6.48 & 3.10 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{DPS guidance sweep on the synthetic out-of-distribution target: $C_p$ correlation,
  affine slope, and drag coefficient all degrade monotonically with guidance weight.}
  \label{tab:dps-sweep}
\end{table}

Correlation falls, the magnitude slope blows up from 3.5 to 6.5, and the body inflates as drag
nearly doubles --- all monotonically with guidance strength. The mechanism is clear: DPS steers
toward the \emph{self-trained} pressure head's idea of the target, but out-of-distribution that
head is unreliable, so stronger guidance merely optimises the surrogate's own error. Plain
classifier-free guidance ($\mathrm{dps} = 0$) is the correct out-of-distribution configuration, and
this rules out a cheap surrogate-only fix.

The amplitude bias itself is model-side, not solver-side. A roughness-, node-density- and
resolution-controlled study shows the affine slope $a$ is unmoved by any CFD or discretisation
lever and is already $\approx 2.8$--3.0 \emph{in-distribution} (against the solver's validated
1.78). This points to a generative amplitude bias --- pressure-head calibration --- to be addressed
model-side with the solver trusted as ground truth, rather than a discretisation artefact.

\subsection{Qualitative flow fields}

Instantaneous OpenLB fields around a generated in-distribution shape, rendered in ParaView, give
the qualitative picture behind the validation numbers. In the velocity-magnitude field the laminar
boundary layer separates off the body and rolls up into a regular von K\'{a}rm\'{a}n vortex street
--- the alternating wake that defines the Re 500--1000 shedding regime --- with accelerated flow
over the shoulders and a low-momentum wake behind the body.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{report_paraview_velocity_wake.png}
  \caption{Velocity-magnitude field around a generated in-distribution shape: a laminar separation
  rolling up into a regular von K\'{a}rm\'{a}n vortex street, with accelerated shoulder flow and a
  low-momentum wake.}
  \label{fig:paraview-velocity}
\end{figure}

The pressure field at the same instant shows a high-pressure stagnation region on the upstream nose
and a train of discrete low-pressure vortex cores shed into the wake. The surface trace of exactly
this field \emph{is} the $C_p(x/c)$ curve that the inverse-design loop grades, making concrete what
the correlation numbers above are measuring.
